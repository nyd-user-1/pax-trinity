`load` applies pending migrations itself and rebuilds the `geographies` table afterwards (`python -m audience.geographies` does it alone). A loader whose raw data is absent prints how to get it and exits 0 without writing. A DuckDB file another process holds is retried every 30 s, up to 20 times.

Set `AUDIENCE_TEST_DATABASE_URL` to also run the Postgres test; it works in a throwaway schema.

## Reruns

- Rows upsert on `natural_key`, the sha256 of source, source_version, geo_code, period_start, period_end, construct, item_text, response_option, subgroup_dim, subgroup_value and estimate_type. A rerun updates rows in place and the count does not change. `norm_id` is uuid5 of the key, so ids survive reruns.
- Each loader owns its `source` + `source_version` slice: rows an earlier run wrote that the current run no longer produces are deleted in the same transaction.
- Raw files are cached. `--refresh` refetches them. `load acs --vintage 2023` adds ACS 2019-2023 as a separate slice.

## Conventions

- `geography`: `US`, `STATE`, `COUNTY`, `TOWN` (MA county subdivision) or `BLOCKGROUP`. `geo_code` is the Census GEOID without its summary-level prefix: `US`, `25` (MA), `25025` (Suffolk County), `2502507000` (Boston), `250250108011` (a block group). The place name is the first sentence of `provenance_note`.
- `pct` is 0-100. `moe` is the Census 90% margin, `se = moe / 1.645`, and a pct row's moe is in percentage points. Sums and shares use the Census approximations in `audience/moe.py`.
- Response options come from `audience/categories.py`, so a construct lines up across sources.
- A construct needs its `concordance` row before any norm row uses it: `upsert_norms` refuses unknown constructs and rows without `provenance_url`.
- `Store` (`audience/db.py`) is the only way to the database. Array columns take Python lists on both backends.

## Loader notes

- ACS: each cell is stored as a count and as a pct of its table's universe: sex and age over the 18+ population (B01001 adult cells), race_eth and citizenship over total population, education over 25+, income over households. Places with no population (the six water-only "County subdivisions not defined") are skipped.
- CVAP: `cvap` is citizens 18+, overall and by race_eth, with pct = share of that group's 18+ population who are citizens. `race_eth` from this source is the composition of CVAP. The release estimates each of its 13 lines separately, so above block-group level the lines need not add to the line-1 total; composition shares divide by the sum of the five groups so they total 100. Where a small town's CVAP exceeds its separately estimated 18+ count, the citizen-share pct is left out and both counts stay. Block groups publish no 18+ count, so they carry counts and composition only. The six "County subdivisions not defined" (county subdivision 00000) are skipped here too: the release gives them noise-level values.
- Survey loaders (CPS, GSS, ANES, CES, WVS) share `audience/survey.py`. pct is the weighted share of respondents giving a substantive answer: don't know, no answer, refused, skipped and not asked are out of the base, while answers the questionnaire offers ("Not sure", "haven't thought much about it", a volunteered "depends") stay in. `n` is the unweighted base. `se` uses the Kish effective n and ignores clustering, so it understates the design-based error; `moe` is 1.645 x se. Subgroup cells with an unweighted base under 50 are not stored, and the topline row of that period names them in its `provenance_note`.
- Attitude constructs get their concordance row from the loader that writes them: the label and one-line definition from `audience/constructs.py`, the loader's own variable names added to `anes_var` / `gss_var` / `ces_var` / `wvs_var`, and a note on how its wording differs. Each wording is its own `item_text`.
- CPS: the Census public-use files, one `source_version` per year. Variable positions and codes come from each year's technical documentation (`techdocs/cpsnov<yy>.pdf`); only the supplement items PES1 and PES2 move between years. Weight PWSSWGT (4 implied decimals): no year publishes a supplement weight. Every year reproduces P20 Table 4b's percent voted and registered (citizen) for the US and MA. Rates follow the Census P20 convention: the base is citizens 18+ in the civilian population, and refused, don't know, no response and supplement non-interviews stay in it as "No response", so "Voted" is the P20 percent voted (citizen). Turnout among those who answered is Voted / (Voted + Did not vote). Voters count as registered. Each pct row has a count row of weighted persons.
- GSS: weight WTSSNRPS from 2004, WTSSPS before, as the 2024 codebook directs (WTSSALL is the historical weight these replace). Only the needed columns of the 7,000-column file are read. `race` is GSS RACE (White / Black / Other, Hispanic origin not separated).
- GSS bulk tier (`audience/bulk.py`, `load_bulk` in the loader): beside the 15 harmonized constructs, every other categorical variable of the cumulative file, read 400 columns at a time. Construct = the mnemonic lowercased; label = the Stata variable label sentence-cased; definition = the label verbatim (the file carries no questionnaire wording, so bulk definitions are labels, not wordings). Options are the value labels in coded order, first letter capitalized; codes labeled refused, don't know, no answer, uncodeable and the like leave the base with Stata's extended missing values. A variable qualifies with 2 to 24 labeled codes and no unlabeled values, except a scale labeled only at its ends (1-7, 0-10), which keeps every integer point, the unlabeled ones named by their number. Continuous variables, administrative and paradata variables (`ADMIN_VARS`, `admin()`), coded open answers, the 15's own variables and mnemonics that collide with an existing construct id are skipped. Toplines for every year asked; the five breakdowns for the latest year asked only. `provenance_url` is the variable's Data Explorer page. `reports/gss_bulk.json` lists what was taken and skipped, with reasons.
- GSS wordings (`audience/loaders/gss_wording.py`): every GSS construct's question text, from the Data Explorer's variable list (the same cached list as the bulk tier's links), in `concordance.gss_wording` (migration 008). The Explorer prints the codebook page, so the step keeps the words a respondent hears and removes what surrounds them, never rewording or recasing: cp1252-garbled punctuation is decoded, a lost character is restored from its position (apostrophe, quotes, or the ellipsis between a stem and its item), item letters go so a battery item reads as its stem joined to its item, interviewer directives go only whole (a bracket opening with a directive word, a capitals sentence carrying one), analysts' notes go, and fills such as `[NAME]` and `[CURRENT STATE]` stay. No wording where the Explorer has none, for headers in capitals, for derived variables whose text says how they were built, for the three harmonized constructs that pool several questions, and for ETHREGION95, whose Explorer text is another variable's. `ERRATA` fixes one misspelling no instrument printed ("Pleae indicate"). Tested against GSS Methodological Report 133 and `gss.ITEMS`; `reports/gss_wording.json` lists every construct's raw text, wording and any reason for none.
- GSS topics (`audience/loaders/gss_topics.py`): every GSS construct's subjects as the Data Explorer files them, verbatim and in the Explorer's order (alphabetical, so the first is not a primary), in `concordance.gss_topics` (migration 010); a tag the source repeats appears once, and a harmonized construct pooling several variables takes all of theirs in order of first appearance. The Explorer's variable list carries `subject` and `module` beside the question text; the step fetches its full records once into `raw/gss/data_explorer_subjects.json`, and for the variables the list leaves without a subject, each variable's own page record into `raw/gss/data_explorer_details.json` (both resumable, half a second between requests, synced to the bucket's `raw/gss/`). For many variables first asked since 2010 the page's subject is only the name of the module that asked it (GSS Next, ISSP Digital Societies); a module isn't a topic, so those are skipped and the question ships without topics, while the raw files keep every module. `reports/gss_topics.json` gives the vocabulary, each tag's count, where each construct's tags came from (the list or the page), the constructs skipped as module names, and the untagged constructs.
- ANES and CES topics (`audience/loaders/anes_topics.py`, `ces_topics.py`): the publisher's own subject tags, verbatim and in its order, in `concordance.anes_topics` and `ces_topics` (migration 011). ANES: the CDF variable list by subject (`raw/anes/topics/`, fetched once), each VCF variable's section and subsection; sections VI and XII print no heading of their own, so their subsections stand alone; the 2024 study's own variables have no subject filing (its documents group by interview phase only) and ship without topics. CES: the cumulative guide's outline (`raw/ces/docs/`), one section per variable, matched by name, so the cumulative tier and same-named yearly variables are tagged and the yearly Common Content otherwise is not (its guides, questionnaires and Dataverse files file nothing by subject). Headings naming the fielding apparatus (ids, weights, administration, the screener, the interviewer) are left out. `reports/anes_topics.json` and `ces_topics.json` give each source's vocabulary, counts and untagged constructs with reasons.
- ANES: weight VCF0009z. `trust_gov` (VCF0604) and the agree/disagree internal-efficacy item (VCF0614) stop in 2012 in the CDF; VCF9252 carries internal efficacy from 2008. The CDF has no national-pride item.
- ANES bulk tier (`audience/loaders/anes_bulk.py`): beside the 13 harmonized constructs, every categorical variable of the cumulative file with 2 to 24 substantive codes, and of the 2024 study where the cumulative file draws on no 2024 source for it. Construct = the variable name lowercased (`vcf0876`, `v241383`); label = the Stata variable label sentence-cased; definition = the label verbatim, not the questionnaire wording. Bands are the value labels in coded order (the codebook's full text where the Stata file cuts one short); a scale labeled only at its ends keeps every integer point, the GSS tier's way. Don't know, refused, NA, the codebook's missing codes, negative codes and stray unlabeled codes held by five or fewer respondents are out of the base; an offered "haven't thought much about it" stays in. Toplines for every study year asked, the five breakdowns for the latest year whose topline is stored. Feeling thermometers come in as means. Skipped and named in `reports/anes_bulk.json`: administrative variables, interview mode and paradata, open-ended codes, counts, continuous variables, derived thermometer averages and indexes, the 30 variables the harmonized constructs read, and from the 2024 study its RESTRICTED variables and the PAPER: duplicates asked of paper respondents alone. Cumulative-file variables take the weight their codebook entry names (VCF0009z; VCF0010z or VCF0011z for the 1970 type-1 and type-2 items); 2024-study variables take V240107a (pre-election) or V240107b (post-election), the weights the ANES variable pages use, and the cumulative file's own subgroup recodes of the same respondents, matched on case id. `provenance_url` is the ANES variable page of the question as last asked (1992 on), else the cumulative codebook opened at the variable. Bulk labels and definitions register from the loader, not `constructs.py`; their coded order goes to `response_order`.
- CES: weights per the CES guide (commonweight, commonpostweight for post-election items, vvweight for validated voters). Validated primary turnout is over all adults, with no voter-file record counted as not voting. The 2024 presidential primary is stored for Massachusetts only: states that held one combined primary record the vote under TS_p2024.
- CES bulk tier (`audience/loaders/ces_bulk.py`, run by `ces.run` after the 7 harmonized constructs): every categorical variable of each year's common content with 2 to 24 labeled categories, and of the cumulative file. The CSVs carry codes only, so the loader range-reads the header and value-label block of each year's Stata release (half a megabyte of the 1 GB file) into `raw/ces/labels/`; the data still come from the CSV. Construct = the variable name lowercased (`cc24_321a`, `newsint`); label = the variable label sentence-cased, piped names shown in brackets (`$CurrentGovName` reads [governor]); definition = the label verbatim, not the questionnaire wording. Bands are the value labels in coded order, first letter capitalized; skipped, not asked, don't know, prefer not to say and NA are out of the base, an offered "Not sure" stays in. Skipped and named in `reports/ces_bulk.json`: administrative variables (ids, weights, consent, device, module, zip, county, district and state codes, timings, open text), variables with no labels or one label, over 24, partly labeled numbers, the candidate-slot items whose categories are per-respondent lookups (`$SenCand1Name`), the variables the 7 read, and ids another source or the seed holds (`sex`). A yearly variable the cumulative file also carries under its name is served from the cumulative alone. Toplines for every year asked, US and MA; the five breakdowns for the latest year asked. Weights: commonweight, or commonpostweight for an item only post-election respondents answered; the cumulative's year-specific weight, or weight_post for post-election items in the years that carry it, as its guide advises. Each slice is its own `source_version` (`CES 2024 Common Content, bulk tier`, `CES Cumulative Common Content 2006-2025`), so the 7's slices are untouched. Bulk labels and definitions register from the loader, not `constructs.py`.
- WVS: wave 7 has no Schwartz value battery; importance of life domains (Q1-Q6) and freedom versus security (Q150) are stored instead. `item_text` is the codebook variable label.
- MA enrollment: the Secretary of the Commonwealth's enrollment breakdowns, one `source_version` per as-of date: the state elections of 2008-2024 and February 1, 2025. `registration_by_party` stores a count and a pct (share of the town's registered voters, `n`) for each column the vintage prints (Democrat, Republican, Unenrolled, and whichever of Libertarian, Green-Rainbow, United Independent Party or Working Families had party status) plus Political Designations, which the town tables omit: it is the town's registered voters less the printed columns, an identity that holds exactly for every county on page 1. The PDFs set figures among dot leaders, so the reader drops the dots and groups words by position, and it checks each file (351 towns, county and statewide sums) before writing. Towns take their GEOID from the geographies table by county and name. The 2006 and older breakdowns use other layouts and are not loaded.
- Lift dataset: one row per study x ad x outcome from the archive's `output/processed_data/regression_ates.RDS`, the ad arm against its study's placebo (lm_robust with the study's pre-treatment covariates). Constructs `lift_favorability`, `lift_vote_choice` and `lift_vote_choice_dichotomized`; `estimate_type` `lift_pp`; `se` as published, `moe` 1.645 x se; period the cycle year. Favorability and vote choice are 0-100 indices, so their lifts are index points; the dichotomized outcome's are percentage points. `response_option` is `<ad id> (study <study id>)`, because one ad was tested in two studies. Each `provenance_note` carries the study's cycle, race level, states, test date, days to the election, n, the published 95% CI and p, and the coders' tone and focus. The archive has no turnout outcome.
- PAX tests: `seed/pax_waves.csv` rows become a `lift_pp` row and a `pct` level row under `lift_<outcome>` (response_option the arm), plus one `Placebo` level row per outcome x subgroup x wave. Outcomes named like the lift dataset's (favorability, vote_choice) share its constructs, so the benchmark pools them.
- Polls: `seed/open_primary_polls.csv` maps 1:1 onto norms, values as given; an empty `n` or `moe` stays NULL, `se = moe / 1.645`, and a row missing `provenance_url` is rejected and named. Each poll or result is its own slice; one that leaves the seed is deleted.
- AP-NORC: Q8 of the May 2016 public-use file, by sex, age group and party, weight finalwt, under the seed topline's construct and `item_text`. The base keeps skipped or refused as its own option, as the topline does. Age is `age_5`, the file's five groups (18-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-64, 65+), which don't match `age`'s buckets; party counts leaners with their party.
- Geographies: `geo_code`, the Census name, level and `parent_code` (state to US, county to state, town and block group to county) for every code in norms, from the cached CVAP and ACS files.
- Test fixtures under `tests/fixtures/` are synthetic and carry `SYNTHETIC` in their names; every loader refuses such a file unless a test passes `allow_synthetic=True`.

## Query layer

`audience/api.py`, read-only (DuckDB opened `read_only=True`, Postgres sessions set read-only; a held write lock is retried every 30 s, up to 20 times):

- `get_norm(construct, geography, geo_code, subgroup_dim=None, year=None)`: rows as dicts. `year` is an int (periods overlapping that year), `(a, b)`, or `"latest"` (each source's most recent period).
- `get_population_targets(geo_code, dims=...)`: `{dim: {category: share}}`, shares summing to 1, from the latest ACS pct rows; `race_eth` from CVAP (citizens 18+), falling back to ACS.
- `get_topic_series(construct)`: POLL and ELECTION_RESULT rows ordered by `period_end`, toplines by default.
- `get_lift_benchmark(outcome_type)`: `{p10, p25, p50, p75, p90, n_ads, cycles}` over LIFT_ARCHIVE and PAX_TEST lifts; `n_ads` counts ad-level estimates.
- `place_lift(lift_pp, outcome_type)`: the percentile of a lift among them (ties count half), with the benchmark and a `caveat`.

## Construct layer

A question is one asked instrument (abany, abanyg, conlegiy), a `concordance` row. A construct groups the versions of one question across rewordings, administrations and sources: a recorded, citable claim that they measure one thing, nothing averaged. `python -m audience.construct_layer` replaces the `construct` and `construct_member` tables (migration 009) with `SEED` in `audience/construct_layer.py`, the whole library, in one transaction. Each construct carries an `anchor`, the citation that makes the grouping the field's (GSS replicating core, MR133), or a `note` saying whose judgment it is. A member is `core` (exactly one per construct), `version` (a rewording or experiment) or `mode` (the same text in another administration, naming its `parent`); members keep the seed's order. The 15 harmonized GSS constructs enter as one-member constructs of themselves. A construct joins the seed only once it has been ruled on.

Past the ruled seed, the layer grows from the field's own trend lists (`audience/canonical.py`): the GSS Data Explorer's Key Trends, 181 items read through the routes its own front end calls, and the ANES Guide to Public Opinion and Electoral Behavior, 225 charts, each mapped to its variables through the Guide's previous edition's table of the same title as the Internet Archive holds it. Each item becomes a construct anchored to it, in `construct_anchor` (migration 012) with the list's own subject and subtopic. An item's variables map to the harmonized id where `gss.ITEMS` or `anes.ITEMS` folds them, else the variable lowercased. Items that share a question are one construct, and an item that shares a question with a seed construct joins it. A Guide chart the previous edition has no table for takes the one ANES question that spans exactly the chart's years and whose label is nearest its title (`SPAN_MATCH`), and a construct anchored only in Key Trends and one anchored only in the Guide whose titles share `CROSS_LIST` of their content words, each the other's only such partner, are one construct. A question asking exactly the same words as a member (the app's normalized wording) joins it as a mode member. What the rules cannot settle (charts without a matching table, items with no question in the store, concepts the two lists may share without sharing a question, CES questions that read like a construct) goes to `reports/construct_layer.json` for a ruling and is never forced.

## Export

`python -m audience.export` writes `public/audience-data/` at the repo root for the `/audience` page: `index.json` (`sources` with label, row count and file; `constructs` with label and definition, `wording` where a GSS question has one, and `topics` where the publisher files a question under a subject; `geographies` at US, STATE, COUNTY and TOWN level) and one `<source>.json` per source, an array of `[construct, geo_code, period_start, period_end, response_option, subgroup_dim, subgroup_value, estimate, estimate_type, moe, item_text, n, provenance_url]`. Block groups are left out; estimates and moe round to 1 decimal and counts to whole numbers; `item_text` is kept for POLL and ELECTION_RESULT only; `n` is null where the store has none; a topline's subgroup fields are null; dates are ISO. A file over 4 MB splits into `<source>-2.json`, each part listed in the index. A cell's response options follow the coded order in `response_order` (migration 007, written by the bulk tier), else the alphabet.

`index.json` also carries `families`, the construct layer: `{id, label, definition, anchor, note?, members: [{question, relation, of?}]}` per construct, members in seed order, a mode member's `of` naming the member it repeats. A member without exported rows is left out; the key is absent while the layer is empty.

Each question is also served alone, so a page lists a source from the index and reads only the question it opens: `q/<source lowercased>/<construct>.json` holds that question's rows from `<source>.json`, same order, same 13 fields, and `index.json`'s `questions` has one entry per file, in the same order: `{source, question, first, last, waves, options}`, the first and last wave years and their count (the distinct years of `period_end` over all its rows) and each response option once in row order. A question that leaves the store leaves `q/`. The whole-source files stay beside them.

## Message-test analysis

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m audience.load ma_results            # the MA 2024 presidential return, statewide and 351 towns
.venv/bin/python -m audience.analyze open-primaries-1   # report to reports/open-primaries-1.json
.venv/bin/python -m audience.analyze --simulate         # validation to reports/simulation.json
```

- Reads every item for the test from the DynamoDB table `pax-trinity-test-responses` (machine credentials), written by the app's collector at `/api/tests/<id>/responses`. `--env production` drops dev walks, which the collector stamps `development`.
- Outcomes are 0/100 per respondent: `support` (Yes on the ballot question) and `comprehension` (the correct description), so lifts are percentage points against the placebo.
- Three estimators per arm: unweighted difference in means; weighted, raked to Massachusetts targets on education (ACS), party (the latest CES party ID, leaners with independents as the entry question asks it) and 2024 vote (the official return over CVAP adults), weights capped at 5x the mean; and covariate-adjusted (Lin 2013, centered entry-question dummies and their interactions with the arm), the headline. Standard errors are HC2. The 90% criterion calls a lift detectable when its 90% interval excludes 0; Holm-adjusted p-values across arms sit beside the unadjusted ones.
- Subgroup lifts per entry question keep every cell and flag those under 50 in either arm. Headline lifts are placed with `api.place_lift` against PAX's own earlier waves and, for support, the lift dataset's dichotomized vote choice.
- The report schema is in the `audience/analysis.py` docstring. A quantity the data cannot support is null with a note saying why.
- The simulator plants lifts of 6, 3, 0 and -2 points, skews education away from the targets so the weights move, and runs the same analysis path many times: 40 runs at 50,000 per arm for bias, 1,000 runs at 500 per arm for 90% coverage.
- The POLL source's export carries the polls registry (`audience/polls_registry.py`): `polls.json` (split like a source), one record per poll behind the rows, with only what its sources state: pollster, sponsors, field dates, place, populations and samples, the margin, mode, fieldwork and weighting its release prints, its links, the questions it asked at their waves, and the keys of its rows. `index.json`'s `polls` lists the files. A row `[..., item_text, n, provenance_url]` belongs to the poll whose `rows` hold `{url: provenance_url, end: period_end, label: item_text}`.
