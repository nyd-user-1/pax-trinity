# Box bodies: the ten PAX boxes as value

## America Thinks

> Every message rests on a guess about what voters already believe. I replace the guess with the record: five decades of the General Social Survey, ANES and CES beside the census, the turnout record and Massachusetts' enrollment rolls, 8,028 questions in all, so you write to where opinion actually stands.

It opens on the partner's real risk (building a message on a wrong assumption about the electorate) and lets the depth of the record, in counts and names, carry the persuasion.

## Chat

> The answer you need is often already on file. Ask me in plain words and I answer from the scores, scripts and shorts on record, and say so when a number isn't there instead of making one up; five questions are free before you sign in.

The value is time saved and trust kept, so it promises plain answers from the record and an honest "not on file", the one thing a partner can't get from a general chatbot.

## Clips

> Good cuts get lost in inboxes and shared drives. All 209 of the partners' videos sit in one library, each with its review thread beside it and, where it has been tested, its measured lift, so you choose what to run from evidence instead of memory.

It names the everyday failure (the strong cut nobody can find) and answers it with the count and the one feature that turns a library into a decision tool: the tested lift beside the video.

## Consensus

> The loudest voice in the room is rarely its majority. People agree or disagree with short statements one at a time, and I find the opinion groups in the votes, so you see where your coalition already agrees and where it splits before you take a position.

The payoff is a bad decision avoided (positioning to the loud minority), and it explains the mechanism in a single clause rather than a tour of the tool.

## Scripts

> A script that tested well should be the next script's starting point, not a file someone has to dig up. This is where scripts will be drafted and revised beside the clips they become; it isn't built yet.

It sells the problem the box exists to solve and keeps an honest tense: there is no Scripts page, and the line says so plainly.

## Lift Benchmarks

> A lift means little until you know what ads usually do. 1,583 effects from campaigns' own randomized experiments (Hewitt, Broockman, Coppock et al., APSR 2024) show the ordinary range, so you can tell a strong result from an average one before you scale it.

Partners overreact to a single number; the line positions the archive as the yardstick that stops them, with its count and its citation as the proof.

## Message Tests

> Most video budgets are spent on instinct. A panel is randomly shown one of your videos or a placebo, then asked how they would vote, so the gap between the two is the video's effect alone, measured before you buy the airtime.

It leads with the money at stake and explains randomization by its result (an effect you can attribute to the video), not by its steps.

## Partners

> Four organizations, one shared library: Voter Freedom, Yes on 3, Unite America and PAX each keep a desk of their videos and review threads, so no one pays to shoot what another desk already made or already tested.

The partners' value is each other's work; naming all four and the cost they avoid makes the desks read as leverage, not a filing scheme.

## Polls

> Before you cite a poll on open primaries, see all of them: thirteen public polls and seven official ballot-measure results, 2014 through the 2026 Massachusetts tracking, side by side in America Thinks, so one favorable number can't stand in for the record.

It warns against the cherry-picked poll, the mistake that costs a campaign its credibility, and points the reader into America Thinks, where Polls lives.

## Reports

> A test is only worth what you can read and defend from it. I turn a finished test into its readout: every video's lift against its own placebo, every subgroup, and what the result can and cannot establish, on one page that prints as a document; the Massachusetts study's two waves and ten videos are there now.

Partners have to justify spend to their own boards; the line sells a defensible readout, then states in the present tense exactly what exists today.
